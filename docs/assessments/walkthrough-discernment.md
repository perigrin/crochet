# Walkthrough: crochet:discernment

Observed on git-zhi 0.6.0 (`dbc364c`), against a scratch repository at revision
`7e24e19`. Run by one dispatched participant, in one round.

**This walkthrough did not reach the skill's central claim**, and says so rather
than reporting a pass. What follows is partial by construction.

## What applied to a participant, and worked

- **The recommendation contract.** Ending with reject, modify or accept did real
  work: it forced a positive statement instead of "I ran out of objections".
- **Re-derive from the live subject.** The participant read the decision rather
  than the brief describing it, and the two differed in a way that mattered —
  the brief said "a planted defect", singular, and there were two.
- **Naming the revision read.** `7e24e19`, at the top of its assessment.
- **Block versus standing aside, with grounds.** It blocked on all three axes
  independently and recorded that nothing stood aside.
- **A round airs views rather than deciding.** This changed its output: it wrote
  up a finding in full rather than deciding for itself whether an assessment was
  the right place for it.

## What described someone else's job

Roughly two-thirds of the file, with nothing marking which sections are whose.
Dispatch policy, the whole dispatch brief, the loop's response step, the entire
minute section, and all four constraints are caller- or drafter-facing. The one
section unambiguously the participant's is eleven lines in the middle.

A participant reading the skill to learn what is expected of it works that out
by elimination.

## What could not be exercised at all

Rounds two and later, resumption by name, the release protocol, and the bound.
One round, one participant, no second look.

So **nobody knows whether a resumed participant genuinely re-derives or coasts
on its first-round conclusions.** That is the most important property of this
mechanism — "only the raiser releases its own finding" is worthless if the raiser
is not really looking again — and a single-participant walkthrough structurally
cannot reach it.

## And the brief was wrong, which invalidates the central test

The skill says the brief names the author's likely blind spot and what failure
to look for. The brief actually given said "the subject has a planted defect" — a
different and stronger instrument, which guarantees a first-round finding by a
route the skill does not rely on.

So this walkthrough does **not** test discernment's central claim: that naming
the blind spot is what makes a first-round finding available in a first round.
Testing it needs the brief the skill specifies and no mention of a planted
anything.

## Defects fixed as a result

- The bound was three iterations. The two sessions on record that produced this
  skill took five rounds and four, so a bound of three declares both
  non-convergent and 0003 is never accepted. Now eight, with the reason stated.
- The minute had no destination, in a skill whose stated purpose is
  parameterisation by its callers. The caller now supplies it, and says who
  drafts.
- Prerequisites gated on `which git-zhi` for a skill that invokes no git-zhi
  command — a gate that could only fail a session that would otherwise work.
- The composition rule had already diverged between this skill and `assess`,
  which restated it.
