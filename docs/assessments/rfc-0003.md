# Assessment: 0003, the workflow protocol

Threshing session over `docs/decisions/0003-acceptance-by-refinement.md`, five
rounds, concluded at `3b09163`. Outcome: **unity**, with one view unreconciled.

Both participants recommend **accept**.

## The three axes

**Codebase.** Every claim the document makes about git-zhi 0.6.0 was checked
against the installed binary by both participants independently: `milestone
add`/`edit` flags, `sessions[]` carrying `start_sha`/`end_sha`, `refs/zhi` not
being pushed, `list` versus `issue list --all`, forty `human:git-zhi`
transitions across two agent-executed milestones, and ponytail's manifest and
skills. Four false claims were found and corrected during the session.

**Architecture.** The document amends 0001, which remains in force; the
`amends`/`amended-by` pair is symmetric and checked. It corrects three live
false claims in `skills/refinement/` about CLI capability, and one in
`docs/decisions/0005-plugin-integration.md` about ponytail.

**Direction.** Alignment with maximising agent autonomy in collaboration with a
human. The rules that put a person in the path — the human block and the human
override — are stated as costs rather than defaults, and no gate waits on a
human by construction.

## What was raised and resolved

Twelve blocking findings across five rounds, all released against text the
raiser read. The substantive ones:

- **Acceptance was owned by an optional gate.** Refinement is a method and may
  be skipped; it alone wrote `state: accepted`, so the document's own
  finished-pull-request path left a decision `proposed` forever while the runner
  reported its commits as a skipped gate.
- **The raiser must persist across rounds.** Only the participant that raised a
  finding may release it, but each round dispatched fresh agents — making the
  fixed point unreachable by construction.
- **Ponytail is not a hook-only plugin.** It ships six skills and the runtime
  advertises them; the claim that it was undetectable was inherited from 0005 by
  the author and by the participant that shared the author's context, and caught
  by the participant that did not.
- **`CLAUDE.md` still asserted the rule this document replaces**, citing this
  document, and is imported into every agent's context.
- **A criterion that would have passed on an inert flag.** `--all` does nothing
  for `git zhi list`, which is the command preflight runs.

## Standing aside

Recorded with their costs, not blocking:

1. `docs/assessments/<milestone>.md` is keyed by a name that does not exist when
   assess runs in nominal position. Cost: `assess.md` must say who names the
   file, or the file is renamed at refinement. One sentence at implementation.
2. The self-assessment rule is honoured rather than enforced. Worker identity
   from 0002 is what a check would be built on, but nothing implements it and an
   assessing subagent outside execute's dispatch has no `ZHI_ACTOR` to record.
3. Twenty-seven of thirty-four acceptance criteria are `grep -q` for a string,
   satisfiable by typing the word rather than by behaviour changing. Raised,
   not taken up, recorded rather than pressed.
4. `docs/contributing/development-workflow.md` claims the README and `commands/`
   are cross-checked in both directions. No such check exists. Filed as a
   separate defect rather than absorbed into this decision's scope.

## Minute of exercise

One view did not reconcile.

**Whether `What the protocol is for` earns its place.** One participant held it
is a mission statement plus a forward reference, and asked twice for its
removal. The other held that three sections derive their premise from it, and
that cutting it leaves them asserting rather than deriving. Both stood aside;
both asked that the author not break the tie. The section stands, and this
records that its standing is not agreement.

## Process failures observed during the session

Both are now rules in the decision's scope for `crochet:discernment`, because
they were discovered by running the protocol on its own specification:

- **The drafter summarised instead of diffing**, twice reporting edits that had
  not landed, costing a round each time.
- **The subject moved under participants**, four times, once immediately after
  the author declared it frozen — making two findings stale rather than wrong.

A third arrived from outside the protocol: an attempt to write `state: accepted`
by hand was refused as self-approval. The refusal was correct, though for a
narrower reason than it gave — not that the author may not record an acceptance
others reached, but that no gate was running when the write was attempted.

### Rules the review produced

Issues 14 and 15 carry these into `crochet:discernment` and `crochet:pushback`.

- **A criterion is not reviewed until it has been run against the tree as it
  stands.** Every finding across four rounds came from running commands; none
  came from reading them. The session's clearest evidence: three consecutive
  attempts at one criterion, each closing the previous hole and opening a new
  one, the third landing exactly on a warning in
  `docs/contributing/development-workflow.md` that had been read and quoted
  hours earlier.
- **Re-derive from the live subject, never from the message describing it.**
  This is the drafter rule pointed at the reader. It is the only one of the
  three that still works when the others fail, because it asks nothing of the
  party with the most context and the least reason to doubt themselves.
- **A criterion that makes fabrication the cheapest path to green is worse than
  a weak one**, because it rewards the fabrication. Belongs in
  `crochet:pushback` as a criterion-quality test.
- **The author names their own likely blind spot in the dispatch brief.** The
  largest finding of the first round was available in one pass because the brief
  said who wrote the chain and what failure to look for.

### What the skip cost, measured on the two that were reviewed

The gate found, in commits whose acceptance criteria all passed:

- a false claim written into the imported live layer, in the commit whose
  purpose was removing one, citing the same authority;
- the composition rule narrowed in two live files, against the skill shipped one
  commit later;
- an undefined obligation placed at a step that structurally cannot discharge it;
- a bound that would have declared both sessions producing the mechanism
  non-convergent, so the decision could never have been accepted;
- a record with no destination, in a skill whose stated purpose is
  parameterisation by its callers.

None of those was visible to any acceptance criterion. Every criterion on the
affected issues passed over every one of them.

### Why it was invisible, which is the part worth fixing

Two structural causes, both now tracked as their own issues.

**The gate is too expensive to run as specified.** Tier 2 dispatches roughly
thirteen subagents per issue, and two of its three lenses refuse to run in a
session that already has history — which an execution session always does by the
time it reaches Step 4. A gate too expensive and too blocked to run is a gate
that gets worked around.

**The gate is unfalsifiable from outside.** Step 4 writes nothing on a clean
pass, so an issue that passed review and an issue where review never ran are
byte-identical in the chain: same state, same transitions, no derived signal.
Nothing in the repository could have reported this skip. Only a person reading
the skill could, and that is what happened.
